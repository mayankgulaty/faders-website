FM radio on iPhone: what actually works

Short answer: iPhones can’t pick up FM broadcasts. There’s no FM tuner that apps can use, and “FM radio” apps on the App Store are internet radio. The good news is that almost every FM station also streams online, with the same programmes at the same time.

1. An internet radio app

Search for your station by name or frequency in a radio app and it plays the station’s live stream, the same as on FM. In Faders you can search by frequency (try “98.1”), see what’s on before you tune in, and browse your country’s stations (in CarPlay, Near You shows the local ones).

2. The station’s own app

Big broadcasters have their own apps. They work, but you end up with one app per station, each with its own adverts and sign-in.

3. A smart speaker or car radio

Your car’s FM and DAB radio still work, of course. To get your phone’s stations in the car, use CarPlay or Bluetooth.

FM or internet: what’s the difference?

  • Coverage: FM fades outside the station’s area; the stream works anywhere with data.
  • Sound: most streams are 128 kbps, close to FM quality, and without static.
  • Delay: streams run 10 to 30 seconds behind FM, which only matters if you’re listening to both at once.
  • Extras: song names, pausing, rewinding and favourites, which FM can’t do.
