How much data does internet radio use?

Streaming radio uses mobile data at a steady rate, set by the station’s bitrate: the higher the bitrate, the better it sounds and the more data it uses. Unlike video, it’s modest.

Data per hour by bitrate

BitratePer hourTypical stations
48 kbps (AAC+)about 22 MBtalk and news, mobile streams
64 kbpsabout 29 MBtalk, many local stations
128 kbpsabout 58 MBmost music stations
192 kbpsabout 86 MBhigher-quality music
320 kbpsabout 144 MBaudiophile streams

So an hour of a typical music station is about the same as five minutes of standard-definition video. A daily one-hour commute at 128 kbps uses about 1.2 GB a month.

How to use less

  • Pick stations with a lower bitrate for talk and news; speech sounds fine at 48 to 64 kbps. Faders shows each station’s bitrate in the player.
  • Use Wi-Fi at home and work.
  • Turn on Low Data Mode (Settings › Mobile Data › Mobile Data Options) if your plan is tight. Faders saves its background song recognition for Wi-Fi.
  • Download your own music for flights and tunnels: Faders plays albums from your Plex or Jellyfin server offline.

Does recognising songs use data?

Very little. When a station doesn’t say what’s playing, Faders sends a short audio signature, not the audio, to name the song. On your favourites’ cards it only does this on Wi-Fi.
